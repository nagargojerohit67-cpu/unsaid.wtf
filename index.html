🏆 AAJ KA VILLAIN AWARD — WEEKLY HONOURS CEREMONY

THIS WEEK'S VILLAIN IS NOT A PERSON.
IT IS AN IDEA. THE IDEA IS "JUST BE CONSISTENT."

Somewhere in India right now, a person with a ring light and seven motivational courses they haven't completed is recording a 47-second video. It will get 2.3 million views. It will say "bas consistent raho." It will help nobody.

"Just be consistent" is the most confidently delivered piece of advice in human history that has never once explained anything. It is the nutritional label on a packet that says "contains food." It is the terms and conditions of wisdom — technically present, functionally useless, skipped by everyone.

Consistent at what, exactly? At waking up at 5 AM? At posting content? At journaling, cold showers, 40 pages a day, 3 litres of water, meditating, exercising, networking authentically, and building in public — all before 9 AM, before the job you need to afford the ring light?

The person giving you this advice wakes up at 5 AM because their job is waking up at 5 AM and telling you to wake up at 5 AM. They found a loop and they are living inside it and charging ₹4,999 for the masterclass.

India produces approximately 500 new consistency gurus per week. Each discovered the secret to success between ages 22 and 26, during a period when they had no rent, no dependents, and parents who asked zero financial questions. Each has a before photo where they look tired, and an after photo where they look tired but in better lighting. The sacrifice is real. The lighting is also very good.

☕ Sunday Slow Read — The One Piece With No Jokes. Just The Thing Nobody Is Saying.
☕ SUNDAY SLOW READ · 24 May 2026

"Everyone Looks Like They Know What They're Doing. Nobody Knows What They're Doing. We Have Agreed Not To Discuss This."

There is a performance happening in India right now and everyone is in it and nobody bought a ticket and the show has no ending. The performance looks like: confidence. Forward motion. A clear answer to "so what are you doing these days?" Delivered smoothly, at family functions and college reunions and chai breaks, by people who rehearsed it on the rickshaw ride over and have not thought past the next sentence.

Everyone is performing certainty. Everyone is watching everyone else perform certainty. And because everyone is performing it so well, everyone assumes they are the only one who is actually uncertain — the only one who doesn't have a plan, who changes their mind every three weeks, who feels, in some quiet 2 AM moment, genuinely lost.

You are not the only one. You are, statistically, the majority.

The myth of the person who has it figured out is India's most successful export that never left the country. It lives in every family WhatsApp group, every LinkedIn feed, every cousin's wedding where someone asks what you're doing and you have to give an answer that fits in one sentence and sounds like a plan even if it is currently a prayer.

Sharma Ji Ka Beta does not actually exist. Or rather: he exists, but he is also lost. He got the rank. He got the job. He is sitting in an office right now, 26 years old, wondering if this is it — he just doesn't tell anyone because the story ended at the rank and there's no template for what comes after. The pressure doesn't end when you succeed. It just upgrades.

The generation currently between 20 and 30 in India is the first one to grow up watching the whole world perform its best version of itself in real time, 24 hours a day, on a device in their pocket. Every generation before this one could fail privately. That distance is gone now. Everything is visible. Everyone is watching. And so everyone is performing.

So here is the thing UNSAID wants to say on this Sunday, plainly, without a punchline: You do not need to have it figured out. Not at 22, not at 25, not at 28.

The people who look like they do — mostly don't. The timeline you think you're behind on was made up by people who were also making it up as they went. The certainty you see in others is, in almost every case, a performance they practiced on the way over.

You are not behind. You are not failing. You are in the middle of something that doesn't have a name yet, doing the best you can with what you know right now — which is exactly what everyone else is doing, even the ones who seem like they aren't. The only difference between them and you is that they've gotten better at the performance. That is not a compliment to them. It is permission for you — to stop.

To say main nahi jaanta without it feeling like a confession. To say "I'm figuring it out" without the shame. To be 24 and uncertain and still completely, legitimately, in the right place. The show doesn't require your certainty. It only requires your presence.
