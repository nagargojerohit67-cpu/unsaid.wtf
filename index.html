📚 PADHAI SLOW READ · 25 May 2026

"Sir Ne Padhaya Nahi, Lekin Exam Time Pe Aa Gaya." — SPPU Pune Postpones Engineering Exams After Student Protests

Savitribai Phule Pune University — which is basically in your backyard if you're reading this in Maharashtra — has postponed engineering exams for SE, TE, and BE students to mid-June. The reason: hundreds of students protested over incomplete syllabus coverage and pending practicals.

Translation: Sir ne padhaya nahi. Exam time pe aa gaya. Classic. The circle of life in Indian engineering education completes another revolution.

The irony of engineering students using protest — a deeply humanities activity — to delay a STEM exam is not lost on us.

This is, at its core, a story about a system that schedules consequences before completing preparations. About students who did everything asked of them — attended lectures when sir showed up, attempted practicals when equipment was available, read notes when notes existed — and still found themselves facing an exam that covered chapters nobody taught.

Meanwhile, parents everywhere are updating their lecture from 'padhai karo' to 'padhai karo PLUS agitation karo' — a truly modern curriculum. The next generation of engineers will graduate with both a degree and firsthand protest experience. One of those skills is more immediately useful. It is not the degree.

Students: 1. System: postponed. Practicals: still pending. Sir: unavailable for comment.

In June, the exams will arrive again. The syllabus will still have gaps. Sir will still be three chapters behind. And somewhere in this cycle is a student who just wants to pass and move on and build something — anything — in a country that keeps scheduling their future while forgetting to teach them how to get there.

The exam will happen. June mein milenge. As always. As ever. The engineers will cope. They always do.
