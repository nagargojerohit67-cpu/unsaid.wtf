"MELODI" RETURNS: MODI GIFTS MELONI MELODY TOFFEES.
COLOSSEUM GOES VIRAL. 110 MILLION VIEWS. SOFT POWER CONFIRMED DELICIOUS.

India has solved foreign policy. The answer, it turns out, was in a pocket-sized candy wrapper all along. Bilateral trade agreements: complicated. A selfie at the Colosseum with an Eclairs in hand: 110 million views and counting.

During Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit to Italy, he gifted Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni a pack of Melody toffees — the childhood candy that has survived every economic crisis, every dietary guideline, and now, apparently, the scrutiny of international diplomacy. Meloni, visibly delighted, posted a selfie video from the Colosseum. The "Melodi" duo was born. The internet, never one to under-react, promptly lost its mind.

110 million views later, political analysts are updating their frameworks. "Soft power" used to mean cultural exports, educational exchange, cinematic reach. India has expanded the definition to include: strategic deployment of glucose-based confections at historic Roman monuments. The Ministry of External Affairs has not confirmed whether this was planned. The Ministry of External Affairs does not need to confirm anything. The numbers speak for themselves.

"We prepared extensively for the summit," said an unnamed diplomat, declining to specify whether this preparation involved a visit to a wholesale candy distributor in Chandni Chowk. The bilateral agenda covered trade, technology, and Mediterranean corridor connectivity. It is remembered, exclusively, for the toffees.

India has officially converted foreign policy into a wholesome Instagram collab. The implications are vast. The calorie count is manageable. The MEA is reportedly now reviewing Alpenliebe for future high-stakes negotiations, with Parle-G reserved as a nuclear option.
