🌍 BRICS BULLETIN

BRICS MEETING IN DELHI ENDS WITHOUT JOINT STATEMENT.
CONSENSUS QUIETLY LEFT THROUGH THE BACK DOOR.

Eleven countries entered the room with the ambition of reshaping the world order. They left with a group photo, a buffet, and an agreement to disagree — which, in diplomatic terms, is called "substantive engagement."

The expanded BRICS foreign ministers' meeting in New Delhi concluded without a joint statement after member countries disagreed over the Middle East and broader geopolitical positioning. India emphasized diplomacy and secure global trade routes. Other members emphasized different things. Nobody agreed on which things. The meeting lasted two days. The disagreement is expected to last considerably longer.

"We remain fully united in our commitment to disagree respectfully," said a diplomatic source who asked not to be named, largely because the quote was embarrassing. The source has since been transferred to a posting in a location with limited wifi.

Analysts noted that building an alternative world order is significantly easier to announce than to coordinate. Announcing requires a microphone. Coordinating requires eleven countries that currently disagree about seven different things to agree about eight. The math is not working out.

Market reaction to the summit: the BRICS acronym remained intact, which was considered a diplomatic success by at least four delegations. The remaining seven delegations had no comment. The remaining seven delegations were also disagreeing about something else.

I READ EVERY DIPLOMATIC STATEMENT FROM THIS WEEK'S SUMMITS.
THE WORD "FRANK" APPEARED 34 TIMES. NOTHING WAS FRANK.

Day 1: BRICS communiqué draft circulated. Word "unity" appears 18 times. Footnote 7 contains three asterisks and no explanation. I investigate the asterisks. The asterisks lead nowhere. This is, I am told, by design. Day 2: Trump-Xi joint readout released. Uses phrase "frank and productive discussions." In diplomatic language, "frank" means someone raised something uncomfortable. "Productive" means they then moved on. Together: "frank and productive" means one country said something the other didn't like, and then they had lunch. Day 4: Norway-India partnership announced. Covers health, space, Arctic, green tech, and "future areas of mutual interest." Future areas of mutual interest is the diplomatic equivalent of "TBD." Day 5: I attend a briefing. A spokesperson uses the phrase "deeply concerned" four times in seven minutes. I count. He does not notice. I am deeply concerned about the word "deeply concerned." Day 7: Conclusion reached. The most important things in global diplomacy are the things nobody says. Welcome to Unsaid.
